# Writing and publishing an article

After the site administrator connects your account, open
[Pages CMS](https://app.pagescms.org/) and use the email invitation to sign in.
You do not need to edit code or use GitHub yourself.

## Start an article

1. Open **Articles** and create a new entry.
2. Add a descriptive **Article title**, choose a **Topic**, and write a short
   introduction of one or two sentences.
3. Choose or upload a **Cover image**, then briefly describe it in
   **Describe the image** for readers who cannot see it.
4. Write the article in **Write your article**. Use headings, paragraphs,
   lists and links as you would in a document editor.
5. Choose the **Article date**, select **Draft**, and save.

The first title creates the permanent article URL. You can edit the title later
without changing the URL. If two articles have the same first title, give the
new article a more specific title before its first save.

The website uses **Dr. Anudeep T Channaiah** as the author automatically. Only
open **Optional settings** when you need a different author, a featured article,
or a different introduction for search results.

## Publish when ready

1. Review the text, cover image, image description and date.
2. Choose **Published — show on the website** under **Publication status**.
3. Save and allow the connected hosting service to finish its build.
4. Open the live blog and check the article.

**Save is not an instant deployment.** The article appears only after a
successful site build. If it does not appear, ask the administrator to check the
latest build; repeatedly saving will not fix a failed build.

## Edit or withdraw

- Saving an edit to a published article updates the live article after the next
  successful build.
- To remove an article from the website, choose **Draft** and save. This keeps
  the article available for future editing.
- To work privately on a replacement version of a published article, first
  discuss the review process with the administrator. Choosing Draft removes the
  current article too; this setup does not keep a separate published revision.

The article date controls its displayed date and ordering. It does not schedule
publication. Drafts are excluded from the website, but their source can still be
read if the GitHub repository is public. The formatted editor is not a live
preview of the website layout; an online draft preview is not configured.
